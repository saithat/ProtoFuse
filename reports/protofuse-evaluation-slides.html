ProtoFuse

Proto programs repeatedly call sequence and structure models while searching for better biological designs. ProtoFuse asks whether recurring groups of objectives can be learned jointly.

Sai Thatigotla and Philip Thomas

PROTOFUSE / EVALUATION · 01 / 05
01

Why ProtoFuse

Joint surrogates reduce repeated parent calls; routing keeps the full models when risk is high.

01 · COST

Repeated model calls dominate.

An optimizer can score thousands of nearby proposals with the same sequence, structure, and binding models. Reusing learned local behavior could reduce time, accelerator use, and credits.

02 · JOIN

Objectives travel together.

The opportunity is to learn recurring groups—not replace one model at a time—so feature work and predictions are shared across the same optimization decision.

What blocks the next claim

Current evidence: 0 hand-crafted negative/OOD challenges exist; high-value positive and positive-but-uncertain cohorts both have zero samples.

G04blocks speed claim

G05blocks scientific claim

G06blocks scope claim

Current evidence: The only surrogate pilot covers one DNA workload and two algebraically simple outputs.

Measure next: Add protein and structure workloads plus unseen target/family/scaffold stress tests.

What to measure next

01

02

Can the router distinguish value from risk?

Train + calibrate

Does one surrogate preserve the joint objective group?

Per-objective error, rank quality, uncertainty calibration, and applicability coverage.

04
Pair executions

Does fusion improve the real pipeline?

Same seed/input/stopping rule; time, steps, parent calls, final regret, and full-model validation.

Selective risk, false acceptance/rejection, deferral recovery, confidence intervals, and net cost.
