Make expensive design loops faster.

Proto programs repeatedly call sequence and structure models while searching for better biological designs. ProtoFuse asks whether recurring groups of objectives can be learned jointly—then used only where a calibrated gate has evidence to trust them.

Current conclusionPROMISING / UNPROVEN

One narrow CPU surrogate is fast and effectively exact. No learned fusion has yet been tested against the expensive GPU parent workloads, paper-matched scores, or positive routing holdouts.

01

Why ProtoFuse

The value is not merely a smaller model. It is fewer repeated parent calls across a joint objective group.

01 · COST

Repeated model calls dominate.

An optimizer can score thousands of nearby proposals with the same sequence, structure, and binding models. Reusing learned local behavior could reduce time, accelerator use, and credits.

02 · JOIN

Objectives travel together.

The opportunity is to learn recurring groups—not replace one model at a time—so feature work and predictions are shared across the same optimization decision.

03 · TRUST

Deferral is part of the design.

Unmatched, uncertain, out-of-distribution, or failed cases must retain the original full-model path. Coverage matters only alongside selective risk.

How trajectories become model splits

01 · runOne seed creates one trajectory

A complete optimizer run emits many sequential proposals whose later states depend on earlier decisions.

02 · groupMany rows remain one unit

Objective rows align into proposal-level teacher samples, but every sample from the trajectory retains one group ID.

03 · splitAssign complete trajectories

Whole groups go to train, calibration, or test. Shuffling proposal rows would leak neighboring optimizer states.

Preferred narrow-workload collection: 60 train + 20 calibration + 20 untouched test trajectories, followed by roughly 50 fresh paired timing trajectories and 40–60 designed challenge cases. Proposal-row counts are larger, but the trajectory counts are the effective independent sample sizes.
